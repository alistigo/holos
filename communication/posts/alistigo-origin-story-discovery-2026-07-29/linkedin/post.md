Last month, Claude did something I hadn't asked for.

I was cleaning up a pile of genealogical notes. Names, dates, half-confirmed relatives scattered across old records. I asked it to help organize the mess.

It answered in text for a minute. Then a panel opened next to the chat: a family tree, built on the spot, shaped from exactly the data I'd handed it. My first reaction was that it was just a nicer table. I was wrong.

I kept going. Cross-checking sources against each other, flagging duplicate entries, asking it to dig into a record that didn't quite match another. Every round, that same panel adapted. By the end it wasn't a rough sketch anymore. It was a real tree, holding everything I'd found, laid out so I could actually read it.

That's when it clicked. Before this, if you wanted something built exactly for your situation, you had two options: a dedicated app for that one job, rare and rigid, or the thing almost everyone reaches for: a spreadsheet or a Word doc, then hours reformatting it, writing a macro, fighting the layout until it finally fit.

Those generic tools are a kind of meta app. A tool for building your own tool, with you doing the building, by hand.

This time, the AI did that part. Live, inside the conversation, shaped to exactly what I needed in that moment.

Has an AI ever built you something you didn't ask for, that turned out to be exactly what you needed?

A few weeks ago, I stopped trusting an AI artifact to be consistent.

Here's what happened.

I gave Claude the same kind of request twice, a few days apart: build me a small tool to track something. Same intent, same data shape. What came back were two different apps. Different layout, different components, different assumptions about what mattered.

Neither one was wrong. That was the problem. I couldn't predict what I'd get, and every rebuild meant more tokens spent and more time steering it back toward what I actually wanted.

I want the flexibility to ask for anything. I don't want the shape of the answer to be a coin flip every time I ask for the same thing.

So I stopped treating that as normal. I started building a library of pieces made specifically for artifacts, so the same kind of request reaches for the same patterns instead of reinventing them, while the content still adapts to whatever I actually need. That became Alistigo.

What surprised me: giving the AI less to invent didn't make it less flexible. It made results cheaper and faster, and for the first time, predictable.

If an AI built the same tool for you twice, would you want it to look the same, or does the variety not bother you?

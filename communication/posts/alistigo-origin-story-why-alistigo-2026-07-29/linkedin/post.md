A few weeks ago, I stopped assuming an AI artifact would just get good on its own.

Here's what happened.

After that family tree Claude built for my genealogy research, I kept pushing it further: more sources, more structure, more ways to view the same data. It kept up, but only because I kept steering it. Every improvement meant another round of me explaining what I actually wanted, and tokens spent rebuilding pieces of interface it had already reinvented once, from scratch, for a problem some library already solved.

The quiet cost was time. Mine, and the model's.

So I stopped treating that as normal. Instead of expecting an AI to invent an interface from nothing in every single conversation, I started building a library of pieces made specifically for artifacts: layouts, data views, the plumbing an agent shouldn't have to figure out fresh each time. That became Alistigo.

What surprised me: giving the AI less to invent didn't make the result less flexible. It made the starting point better, so the effort goes into fitting my actual need instead of the scaffolding around it.

A good default isn't a constraint. It's what lets the AI spend its attention on the part that's actually yours.

What would you want an AI to stop reinventing every time it builds something for you?
